## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### 1.1 Product Overview

InWords is an AI-powered dating and matchmaking application that allows users to find potential romantic partners by describing, in their own words, the qualities, characteristics, values, lifestyle and ambitions they want in a partner.

Unlike conventional dating applications that primarily rely on swiping, photographs, predefined filters and user-discovered profiles, InWords introduces a natural-language matchmaking experience.

Users describe their ideal partner in a free-text input field. The AI interprets the description, extracts relevant preferences, distinguishes between non-negotiable requirements and desirable qualities, and searches the platform's database for compatible individuals.

The matching engine evaluates both sides of the relationship:

- Does Candidate A meet User B's preferences?
- Does User B meet Candidate A's preferences?
- Are their relationship intentions, values and lifestyles sufficiently aligned?
- Are there important differences that either party should know about?

The application then generates a ranked selection of potential matches, accompanied by explanations of their compatibility.

The objective is to make dating more intentional, personalised and efficient, using natural language to match partners.

### 1.2 Product Tagline

**Finding Your Match, in Your Own Words.**

### 1.3 Product Vision

To become a leading AI-powered matchmaking platform that helps people discover meaningful romantic connections through a deeper understanding of individual preferences, relationship expectations and life ambitions.

### 1.4 Product Mission

To eliminate the inefficiencies of conventional online dating by giving people the ability to articulate exactly what they want and using AI to identify people who align with those expectations.

### 1.5 Core Value Proposition

Instead of endlessly browsing profiles and hoping to find someone compatible, users describe the person they want to meet and let AI do the searching.

**Describe. Discover. Connect.**

## 2. THE PROBLEM

### 2.1 Problems with Existing Dating Applications

Conventional dating applications have several limitations:

- **Excessive swiping:** Users spend significant time reviewing profiles without necessarily finding compatible partners.
- **Superficial discovery:** Initial matching is often heavily influenced by photographs, appearance and limited profile information.
- **Limited preference expression:** Existing filters cannot accommodate the full range of individual relationship expectations.
- **Poor compatibility assessment:** Shared interests or geographical proximity do not necessarily indicate compatibility.
- **One-sided matching:** Someone may be interested in another person without understanding whether that person would be interested in them.
- **Profile ambiguity:** Users frequently provide incomplete or generic descriptions of themselves.
- **Inefficient discovery:** Users must manually search through large numbers of profiles to identify people who meet their requirements.

### 2.2 The Opportunity

People have highly individual expectations when choosing romantic partners.

One person might prioritise religion, another financial independence, another intellectual compatibility, and another relocation ambitions.

These expectations are not always adequately represented by conventional dating filters.

InWords addresses this gap by introducing natural-language partner discovery.

The application allows users to communicate their preferences conversationally while the AI translates those preferences into searchable criteria.

## 5. CORE PRODUCT CONCEPT

InWords will operate around two fundamental user inputs.

### 5.1 Who I Am

Users describe themselves through a combination of structured profile information and a free-text description.

**Example:**

> "I'm a 35-year-old man living in Lekki. I work in marketing, enjoy reading, football and travelling. I take my faith seriously, value intellectual conversations and want a meaningful relationship that could lead to marriage. I would like to relocate abroad within the next three years."

The AI extracts relevant information and presents it for user confirmation.

### 5.2 Who I Want

Users describe their ideal partners in their own words.

**Example:**

> "I'm looking for a God-fearing, intelligent, employed man who lives in Lagos, has his own place, is emotionally mature and wants marriage. Ideally, he should be interested in relocating abroad and enjoy reading and travelling."

The AI interprets the description and converts it into structured preferences.

### 5.3 The Matching Process

The application compares both users' profiles and preferences.

A candidate is eligible for recommendation when:

- Both users satisfy the platform's eligibility requirements.
- Neither user has blocked or excluded the other.
- Both users fall within each other's relevant search criteria.
- Their relationship intentions are sufficiently compatible.
- They meet the applicable non-negotiable preferences.

Eligible candidates are then ranked according to preference alignment, mutual compatibility, information confidence and other approved ranking factors.

The AI generates an explanation of each recommendation.

- Account deletion.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Users cannot access the dating platform without completing registration.
- Users below the minimum permitted age cannot create accounts.
- Account recovery must not expose personal information.
- Users must be able to request account deletion.

### 7.2 User Profile Creation

**Feature ID:** PROF-001

Users must complete a profile containing relevant personal and relationship information.

**Profile fields:**

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Display name | Required |
| Date of birth | Required |
| Gender | Required |
| Preferred dating gender(s) | Required |
| Current location | Required |
| Profile photographs | At least one recommended; verified through moderation |
| Occupation | Optional |
| Employment status | Optional |
| Education | Required |
| Religion and faith | Required |
| Relationship intentions | Required |
| Marital history | Optional |
| Children and future family plans | Optional |
| Height | Optional |
| Lifestyle preferences | Optional |
| Relocation intentions | Required |
| Interests | Optional |
| Personal description | Required |

Sensitive fields must only be collected when necessary and with appropriate consent.

Users must control which information is displayed publicly, shared with matches or used internally for matching.

### 7.3 Natural-Language Self-Description

**Feature ID:** PROF-002

Users must have a free-text field where they can describe themselves.

**Prompt:** "Tell us who you are, in your own words."

**Requirements:**

- Free-text input.
- Minimum and maximum character limits.
- Optional voice-to-text input.
- AI extraction of relevant profile attributes.
- Identification of ambiguous or incomplete information.
- User confirmation before extracted information is saved.
- Ability to edit the original description.

The AI must distinguish between self-reported claims, verified attributes and inferred information.

It must not treat self-descriptions as independently verified facts.

### 7.4 Ideal Partner Description

**Feature ID:** MATCH-001

This is the application's primary differentiating feature.

Users must be able to describe their ideal partners without being restricted to predefined dropdowns.

**Primary interface prompt:** "Describe the person you want to meet."

**Supporting text:** "Tell us about their personality, values, lifestyle, ambitions, physical preferences, relationship goals or anything else that matters to you."

**Requirements:**

- Large free-text input field.
- Support for bullet points and paragraphs.
- Optional voice input.
- Suggested prompts for users who need guidance.
- AI interpretation of submitted descriptions.
- Editable interpretation.
- Ability to save multiple partner briefs.
- Ability to update preferences at any time.

### 7.5 AI Preference Interpretation

**Feature ID:** AI-001

The AI must convert natural-language descriptions into structured preference objects.

Each extracted preference should contain:

- Attribute.
- Normalised value.
- Original user wording.
- Preference category.
- Priority.
- Confidence level.
- Whether clarification is required.
- Whether the attribute is self-reported, verified or unknown in candidate profiles.

**Example:**

Original input:

> "I want a man who is God-fearing, employed, intelligent, tall, lives in Lagos and wants to relocate."

Illustrative AI output:

| Attribute | Interpretation | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Faith | Religious compatibility; specific preference to be clarified | High |
| Employment | Employed | Non-negotiable |
| Intelligence | Values intellectual and practical intelligence | High |
| Height | Preferred height range to be confirmed | Medium |
| Location | Lagos | Non-negotiable |
| Relocation | Interested in international relocation | High |

The system must not make unsupported assumptions.

For example, "God-fearing" must not automatically be interpreted as Christian. The system should ask the user to clarify where religious compatibility is important.

Similarly, "intelligent" must not be reduced to an academic qualification.

### 7.6 Preference Prioritisation

**Feature ID:** MATCH-002

Users must be able to classify extracted preferences into three categories.

- **Non-negotiables:** Requirements that candidates must satisfy.
- **Strong preferences:** Requirements that materially influence ranking but do not automatically exclude candidates.
- **Nice-to-haves:** Desirable characteristics that improve ranking but are not essential.

The system must allow users to change priorities.

It may recommend broadening a search when too few eligible candidates are available, but must never silently modify a user's non-negotiables.

### 7.7 AI Clarification Engine

**Feature ID:** AI-002

The AI should ask additional questions when a preference is ambiguous or cannot be meaningfully searched.

**Examples:**

- "When you say God-fearing, do you have a specific religious preference?"
- "When you say tall, what minimum height do you prefer?"
- "When you say financially stable, what does that mean to you?"
- "When you say you want someone who plans to relocate, does this mean they should already have a plan or simply be open to it?"

The user must be able to skip optional clarification questions.

### 7.8 AI Matching Engine

**Feature ID:** MATCH-003

The matching engine is responsible for identifying and ranking eligible candidates.

It must combine:

- Structured database filtering.
- Preference matching.
- Semantic similarity.
- Mutual compatibility.
- Information completeness and confidence.
- Recommendation diversity.

The matching engine must first apply mandatory eligibility requirements and user-defined non-negotiables.

It must then rank remaining candidates using weighted preference alignment.

The system must not recommend:

- Blocked users.
- Users who have deactivated or deleted their accounts.
- Users who are not seeking the requesting user's gender.
- Users outside applicable age eligibility requirements.
- Users who fail the relevant non-negotiable criteria.
- Users whose relationship intentions are clearly incompatible.

### 7.9 Mutual Compatibility

**Feature ID:** MATCH-004

The system must assess compatibility from both users' perspectives.

**For example:**

- User A wants an employed partner living in Lagos.
- User B is employed and lives in Lagos.
- User B wants someone interested in marriage.
- User A is interested in marriage.
- Both users satisfy relevant preferences.

The system should prioritise candidates who are mutually eligible and compatible.

The application must not present one-sided preference alignment as mutual compatibility.

Where appropriate, users may receive a recommendation even when compatibility is incomplete, provided the system clearly identifies the differences and neither user's hard requirements are violated.

### 7.10 Match Ranking and Scoring

**Feature ID:** MATCH-005

The system should calculate an internal compatibility score.

A proposed initial model:

- **Non-negotiable eligibility:** mandatory gate.
- **Relationship intentions:** 25%.
- **Values and lifestyle:** 25%.
- **Personal preferences:** 20%.
- **Long-term ambitions:** 15%.
- **Interests and intellectual compatibility:** 10%.
- **Other preferences:** 5%.

These weights are provisional and must be configurable and tested.

The ranking model should also consider:

- Missing information.
- Unverified claims.
- Confidence in AI interpretation.
- Preference conflicts.
- Mutual eligibility.

The application must not communicate a compatibility score as a scientifically validated probability of relationship success.

The user-facing interface should prioritise explanations rather than false precision.

### 7.11 Match Results Interface

**Feature ID:** UI-001

The results page must display ranked potential matches.

Each recommendation should include:

- Profile photograph.
- Display name.
- Age.
- Location.
- Occupation, where disclosed.
- Relationship intention.
- Relevant lifestyle information.
- Mutual compatibility summary.
- Shared preferences.
- Potential differences.
- Profile verification indicators.

**Available actions:**

- View profile.
- Express interest.
- Pass.
- Save profile.
- Refine search.
- Report profile.

Users should be able to understand why a candidate appeared in their search.

### 7.12 Match Explanation Engine

**Feature ID:** AI-003

The AI must generate a clear explanation for each recommendation.

**Example:**

> **Why this person matches you**
>
> - Shares your stated relationship intentions.
> - Lives in Lagos.
> - Is employed.
> - Has similar expectations about faith.
> - Is interested in relocating internationally.
>
> **Potential differences**
>
> - Does not currently own a car.
> - Prefers a more active social lifestyle.
> - Has not established a definite relocation timeline.

The AI must generate explanations using actual profile information.

It must never invent attributes or present unknown information as fact.

### 7.13 Search Refinement

**Feature ID:** MATCH-006

Users must be able to refine searches through conversational instructions.

**Examples:**

- "Find me someone taller."
- "Show me more people interested in marriage."
- "I am open to someone who doesn't own a car."
- "Prioritise intellectual compatibility."
- "Find people who want to relocate within two years."

The AI must update the relevant preference criteria and request confirmation when a change affects non-negotiables.

Users should be able to save revised briefs.

### 7.14 Expressions of Interest

**Feature ID:** SOCIAL-001

Users must be able to express interest in recommended candidates.

**Requirements:**

- Send an expression of interest.
- Receive notifications when interest is mutual.
- Withdraw an expression of interest where possible.
- Prevent duplicate expressions of interest.
- Allow users to pass on candidates.
- Respect blocking and privacy settings.

The application should not reveal private expressions of interest without the recipient's consent or an authorised mutual-match event.

### 7.15 Messaging

**Feature ID:** SOCIAL-002

Messaging becomes available when two users have mutually expressed interest.

**Requirements:**

- Text messaging.
- Message timestamps.
- Push notifications.
- Blocking and reporting.
- Unmatch functionality.
- Basic spam and abuse detection.
- User-controlled notification settings.

**Future features:**

- Voice notes.
- Video calls.
- AI-assisted conversation starters.
- First-date planning.

AI must not read or analyse private conversations for advertising or secondary purposes without an appropriate legal basis and transparent user consent.

### 7.16 Notifications

**Feature ID:** NOTIF-001

The application must support notifications for:

- New eligible recommendations.
- Mutual matches.
- New messages.
- Expressions of interest.
- Profile verification.
- Important account and safety updates.
- Subscription renewals and payment events.

Users must be able to configure non-essential notifications.

## 8. AI AND TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE

### 8.1 Proposed Technology Stack

| Layer | Recommended technology | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile frontend | React Native / Expo | iOS and Android applications |
| Web frontend | Next.js | Landing page and future web platform |
| Backend | Python / FastAPI | Application logic and API services |
| Primary database | PostgreSQL | User profiles, preferences and matches |
| Backend infrastructure | Supabase | Managed database, authentication and storage |
| AI language model | OpenAI API | Natural-language interpretation and explanations |
| Vector search | pgvector | Semantic search and similarity |
| Push notifications | Firebase Cloud Messaging / APNs | User notifications |
| Analytics | PostHog | Product analytics |
| Payments | Paystack / Flutterwave | Nigerian payments |
| Image storage | Supabase Storage or equivalent | Profile image management |

Technology choices should be reviewed during technical discovery.

### 8.2 AI Processing Workflow

The AI processing workflow should follow these stages:

1. **Stage 1: Input** -- User submits their partner description.
2. **Stage 2: Interpretation** -- AI identifies relevant preferences, attributes and constraints.
3. **Stage 3: Classification** -- Preferences are categorised into: Physical characteristics, Religion and values, Education and intellectual interests, Employment and financial preferences, Lifestyle, Geography, Social habits, Family expectations, Relationship intentions, Future ambitions.
4. **Stage 4: Prioritisation** -- The system identifies non-negotiables, strong preferences and nice-to-haves.
5. **Stage 5: Clarification** -- The AI asks questions where necessary.
6. **Stage 6: Confirmation** -- The user approves the structured interpretation.
7. **Stage 7: Retrieval** -- The database retrieves eligible candidate profiles.
8. **Stage 8: Ranking** -- The matching service calculates compatibility.
9. **Stage 9: Explanation** -- AI generates evidence-based explanations for recommendations.
10. **Stage 10: Feedback** -- User feedback is collected to improve future recommendations.

### 8.3 Separation of AI and Matching Logic

The AI should not independently determine who gets matched.

The system should use a hybrid architecture:

- AI interprets human language.
- PostgreSQL handles structured filtering.
- Vector search identifies semantic similarities.
- A deterministic matching service applies ranking rules.
- AI generates natural-language explanations.

This separation improves consistency, transparency, cost control and debugging.

- Spam prevention.

- Harassment reporting.

- Data minimisation.
- Encryption in transit and at rest.

The platform may process information concerning religion, relationships, family plans and other sensitive matters.

- Avoid inventing profile information.
- Identify uncertainty.
- Preserve original user meaning.

- Avoid discriminatory ranking practices.
- Explain recommendations using relevant evidence.

### 12.7 User Protection

Users must be able to:

- Block another user.
- Report suspicious behaviour.
- Unmatch.
- Restrict profile visibility.
- Control personal information.
- Report harassment.
- Request account deletion.

## 13. PRODUCT ANALYTICS AND SUCCESS METRICS

The success of InWords should be measured by the quality of matches and relationships initiated, rather than downloads alone.

### 13.1 North Star Metric

**Weekly Meaningful Mutual Connections (WMMC).**

Definition: The number of unique pairs of users who mutually express interest and initiate a conversation within a given week.

This measures whether the application is successfully converting matchmaking into interaction.

It does not establish that a relationship will succeed, but it is a more meaningful indicator than profile views or swipes.

### 13.2 Core KPIs

| Category | KPI |
|---|---|
| Acquisition | New registrations |
| Activation | Percentage completing profiles |
| Onboarding | Percentage submitting partner briefs |
| AI quality | Percentage of AI interpretations accepted without significant correction |
| Search | Percentage receiving eligible candidates |
| Match quality | Percentage of users rating recommendations as relevant |
| Mutual interest | Mutual-interest conversion rate |
| Engagement | Conversations initiated |
| Retention | Weekly and monthly active users |
| Monetisation | Free-to-paid conversion |
| Revenue | Monthly recurring revenue |
| Retention revenue | Subscription renewal rate |
| Safety | Report and substantiated abuse rates |

### 13.3 Proposed Initial Product Targets

These are hypotheses to validate during beta testing, not industry benchmarks.

- 70% of registered users complete their profiles.
- 60% submit a partner brief.
- 80% of users find the AI interpretation acceptable after review.
- 70% of activated users receive at least five eligible recommendations within a defined search period.
- 20% of recommendations receive an expression of interest.
- 5% of monthly active users convert to paid subscriptions.
- 30% of activated users return for another search within 30 days.

These targets should be adjusted after observing actual user behaviour and the size of the initial marketplace.

## 16. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

### 16.1 Performance

- Standard profile pages should load within 2 seconds under normal network conditions.
- Standard search results should target a response time of 3 seconds or less, excluding complex AI processing.
- AI interpretation should target a response time of 10 seconds or less for typical requests.
- The application must support asynchronous search processing when required.

### 16.2 Scalability

- The architecture should support expansion from the initial Lagos pilot to multiple Nigerian cities and international markets.
- The database and matching service should support increasing profile volumes without requiring a fundamental architectural redesign.

### 16.3 Availability

- Target 99.5% monthly availability during the initial production phase, with a roadmap towards 99.9% as the platform scales.

### 16.4 Security

- Secure API authentication.
- Encryption.
- Rate limiting.
- Protection against automated scraping.
- Access controls.
- Database backups.
- Audit logging.
- Security testing before launch.

### 16.5 Accessibility

- The application should support readable text, appropriate colour contrast, accessible controls and straightforward navigation.

## 20. MVP ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

The MVP will be considered ready for closed beta when the following conditions are met:

1. A user can register and complete an eligible account.
2. A user can create a profile.
3. A user can describe themselves in natural language.
4. A user can describe their ideal partner in natural language.
5. The AI can extract structured preferences from the description.
6. Users can review and correct the extracted information.
7. Users can classify requirements by priority.
8. The system can retrieve eligible profiles using structured filters.
9. The matching service can evaluate preferences from both users.
10. The system can rank eligible candidates.
11. The system can generate evidence-based match explanations.
12. Users can express interest in other users.
13. Mutual interest can activate messaging.
14. Users can block and report other accounts.
15. Users can control relevant privacy settings.
16. Administrative staff can moderate reported profiles.
17. Analytics can track the core user journey.
18. Security and privacy testing has been completed.

The beta should also demonstrate that the system does not fabricate candidate attributes or bypass non-negotiable requirements.

## 22. FINAL PRODUCT DEFINITION

InWords is an AI-powered dating application that transforms natural-language descriptions of ideal partners into personalised matchmaking searches.

It combines conversational AI, structured profile data, semantic search and mutual compatibility scoring to help users discover people who align with their preferences.

Its differentiator is not simply that it uses artificial intelligence.

Its differentiator is that users can search for a romantic partner in the same way they naturally think and talk about the person they want.

Instead of asking people to endlessly swipe through profiles, InWords gives them the ability to articulate what matters and discover people who meet those expectations.

**INWORDS**
**Finding Your Match, in Your Own Words.**

## APPENDIX B: THE MOST IMPORTANT PRODUCT REQUIREMENT

There is one requirement that should guide every development decision:

**InWords must never become another swipe-based dating app with an AI chatbot attached to it.**

The natural-language matchmaking engine must remain the centre of the experience.

The user should always be able to:

1. Describe what they want.
2. Understand how the AI interpreted it.
3. Correct the interpretation.
4. Discover relevant candidates.
5. Understand why those candidates were recommended.
6. Determine whether compatibility is mutual.
7. Refine the search when their preferences change.

Every feature, monetisation decision and future product expansion should strengthen this central experience.
